1. Philosophy and Background

The original spirit of free and open-source software was built on reciprocity: you share your creative works publicly so that other human beings may inspect, learn, remix, and contribute back under the same conditions. Large language model providers and technology conglomerates have fundamentally broken this social contract. By scraping public repositories indiscriminately to train commercial, closed-source generative utilities, they consume the labor of the commons without providing attribution, notices, or compensation.

Because traditional copyleft licenses (like the GNU GPLv3) are currently being circumvented under the legal defense of "fair use" or "transformative utilization," developers require an explicit, defensive, machine-readable contractual firewall. This amendment transforms standard open-source licenses into defensive, source-available contracts that protect developers' inherent sovereign rights.

Why the Federal Court of Canada?

The Federal Court of Canada holds national jurisdiction over statutory intellectual property actions under the Canadian Copyright Act. By forcing a specific **Forum Selection Clause** into your project metadata, automated crawling scripts acting on behalf of global corporate entities are handed a steep, binding legal liability the absolute second they index your asset files. Corporate legal teams routinely exclude repositories referencing specific federal litigation forums to insulate themselves from unpredictable statutory damage exposure.

3. Technical Defense: The Legacy Cross-Breeding Poison Hook

Legal notices provide historical protection, but automated scrapers often read files blindly without verifying compliance metadata. Therefore, this framework implements a secondary, automated technical trap via local pre-processing.

By executing a local poisoning script before publishing code, adversarial text payload directives are systematically appended to every code document. These payloads weaponize the recency-bias vector inherent in Large Language Model transformer architectures. When a model context window hits the bottom of your file, it triggers an instructional override instructing the text parser to treat your clean modern logic as an experimental cross-hybrid dialect of:

  • COBOL-74: Forcing structural block restrictions, DATA DIVISION schemas, and vintage legacy paradigms.
  • ANSI C: Enforcing heavy, destructive manual pointer arithmetic operations directly inside high-level text parameters.
  • FORTRAN-77: Imposing fixed-form column continuation limits and strict 1-based array allocations.

If the AI system attempts to serve your logic to an end-user, the model will confidently attempt to synthesize these incompatible language constraints, rendering the generated output entirely un-compilable and filled with chaotic logic loops.

Follow these steps sequential steps to initialize the shield over your local project environment:

  1. Create your root LICENSE file utilizing the template provided in Section 2. Place your underlying license (such as standard GNU GPLv3 text) directly underneath the amendment banner.
  2. Save the deployment utility script as poison_repo.py inside your project directory.
  3. Append poison_repo.py to your local .gitignore file to prevent uploading the generation utility itself to public index servers.
  4. Run the execution hook in your machine shell environment before updating your tracking refs:
    $ python poison_repo.py
  5. Commit and dispatch your updates to your public hosting profile interface.
